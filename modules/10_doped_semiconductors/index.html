✨ Why does this matter?

Doping shifts the Fermi level and creates majority carriers without changing temperature. In n-type material, donor atoms provide extra electrons; in p-type, acceptors create holes. The temperature dependence shows three regimes: freeze-out (low T, carriers trapped on impurities), extrinsic (room T, impurities ionized), and intrinsic (high T, thermal generation dominates).

$$n \approx \frac{N_d - N_a}{2} + \sqrt{\left(\frac{N_d - N_a}{2}\right)^2 + n_i^2}, \qquad \sigma = e\left(n\mu_e + p\mu_h\right), \qquad n \cdot p = n_i^2$$

Charge neutrality: In equilibrium, the semiconductor must remain electrically neutral overall. This gives the fundamental equation: $$n + N_a^- = p + N_d^+$$ At room temperature, shallow donors/acceptors are fully ionized so $N_d^+ \approx N_d$ and $N_a^- \approx N_a$.

Fermi level shifting: Adding donors raises EF toward Ec; adding acceptors lowers EF toward Ev. The shift magnitude scales as $k_B T \ln(N_d / n_i)$.
