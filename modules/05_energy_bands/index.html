Energy Bands & DOS

From 3D Bulk to Carbon Allotropes. See how structural confinement reshapes the electronic state.

📖 The Story — From Atoms to Bands to Materials

A single atom has sharp energy levels. Bring atoms together in a crystal and those levels blur into energy bands — continuous ranges of allowed electron energies separated by forbidden gaps. The width of the bands, the size of the gaps, and the shape of the density of states determine whether a material is a conductor, a semiconductor, or an insulator. This module lets you explore how dimensionality — from 3D bulk crystals down to 0D quantum dots — reshapes the electronic structure, and how carbon arranges itself into radically different allotropes: graphene, nanotubes, and fullerenes.

🧩 The Big Idea

In a 3D crystal, the number of states per unit energy g(E) rises as √E near a band edge. Confine the crystal to 2D and g(E) becomes a staircase. Confine it further to 1D and g(E) diverges — these are the Van Hove singularities. In 0D, you get discrete spikes: the quantum dot limit. The playground lets you sweep through all four dimensionalities and watch the DOS morph in real time.

🔬 Why Carbon Is Special

Carbon's four valence electrons let it form sp² (graphene, graphite) or sp³ (diamond, fullerene) bonds. In graphene the pz orbitals form a linear Dirac cone — electrons behave as if massless, moving at effective speed vF ≈ 10⁶ m/s. Roll graphene into a nanotube and the periodic boundary quantises the transverse momentum, creating a 1D band structure. Wrap it into a fullerene and you reach 0D: discrete molecular levels.

🎓 Theory — Tight-Binding, DOS, and Dimensionality

The tight-binding model approximates the electronic band structure of a crystal by assuming that electrons hop between nearest-neighbour atomic orbitals with amplitude t. In a 1D chain of lattice constant a, the dispersion is:

$$E(k) = \varepsilon_0 - 2t\cos(ka)$$

where \(\varepsilon_0\) is the on-site energy. The bandwidth is \(W = 4t\) and the bandgap (if the two atoms in the unit cell have different on-site energies \(\Delta\varepsilon\)) is \(E_g = 2|\Delta\varepsilon|\).

1. Density of States by Dimension

The DOS g(E) counts states per unit energy. Near a parabolic band edge \(E_c\) in d dimensions:

$$g(E) \propto (E - E_c)^{d/2 - 1}$$

3D: \(g(E) \propto \sqrt{E - E_c}\) — smooth onset.   2D: \(g(E) = \text{const}\) — step function.   1D: \(g(E) \propto 1/\sqrt{E - E_c}\) — Van Hove singularity.   0D: discrete delta spikes.

2. Effective Mass

Near a band minimum the dispersion is approximately parabolic. Matching \(E(k) \approx E_c + \hbar^2 k^2 / (2m^*)\) to the tight-binding curvature gives:

$$m^* = \frac{\hbar^2}{2ta^2}, \qquad \frac{1}{m^*} = \frac{1}{\hbar^2}\frac{\partial^2 E}{\partial k^2}$$

Narrow bands (small t) give heavy effective masses; wide bands (large t) give light masses. This is why high-mobility semiconductors have large bandwidths.

3. Dirac Dispersion in Graphene

Near the K point of graphene's honeycomb lattice, the pz bands cross linearly:

$$E(\mathbf{q}) = \pm \hbar v_F |\mathbf{q}|, \qquad v_F \approx 10^6 \;\text{m/s}$$

The effective mass is zero — electrons behave as massless Dirac fermions. The DOS vanishes linearly: \(g(E) \propto |E|\). This gives graphene its remarkable conductivity, broadband absorption (\(\pi\alpha \approx 2.3\%\)), and half-integer quantum Hall effect.

4. ARPES: Probing the Occupied States

Angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) measures the intensity of electrons emitted from a crystal as a function of energy E and emission angle θ (which maps to crystal momentum k). Because only occupied states can emit electrons, ARPES directly visualises the band structure for E < EF. The Fermi surface appears where bands cross EF.

5. Carbon Allotropes as Dimensional Reductions

Carbon is unique: the same element gives 3D diamond (insulator, Eg ≈ 5.5 eV), 2D graphene (zero-gap semimetal, Dirac cones), 1D nanotubes (metal or semiconductor depending on chirality), and 0D fullerenes (discrete molecular levels). Each is a dimensional reduction of the sp² sheet: rolling it up quantises the transverse momentum, wrapping it into a sphere quantises all directions. The playground lets you explore all four from a single starting point.

📖 Lab Notes: Dimensionality & DOS

The Density of States, \(g(E)\), counts the number of available electronic states per unit energy per unit volume.

1. 3D Bulk: \(g(E) \propto \sqrt{E - E_c}\) — parabolic dispersion near the conduction-band edge.
2. 2D Layer (Quantum Well): \(g(E)\) is a step-function — constant for each sub-band.
3. 1D Wire (Quantum Wire): \(g(E) \propto 1/\sqrt{E - E_c}\) — Van Hove singularities at sub-band edges.
4. Carbon Allotropes: Graphene → linear \(g(E) \propto |E|\); CNT → spiky Van Hove peaks; Fullerene → discrete delta-like spikes.
