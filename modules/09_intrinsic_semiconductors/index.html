✨ Why does this matter?

At 0 K, a semiconductor is a perfect electrical insulator: the valence band is filled and the conduction band is empty. At finite temperature, thermal energy promotes electrons across the band gap Eg, creating equal numbers of electrons and holes. Because carrier density depends exponentially on temperature (n ∝ exp(-Eg/2kBT)), conductivity rises dramatically with heating — unlike metals, where phonon scattering dominates.

$$n \approx N_c \exp\left(-\frac{E_c - E_F}{k_B T}\right), \qquad \sigma = e\left(n\mu_e + p\mu_h\right), \qquad E_F \approx E_v + \frac{E_g}{2}$$

Mass-action law: n·p = ni². In intrinsic material, n = p = ni. Doping breaks this symmetry and shifts the Fermi level.

Optical excitation: Photons with hν ≥ Eg can create electron-hole pairs directly. This is the basis of photodetectors and solar cells.
