Carrier Density vs Temperature ⓘ
Physics behind this curve
At low T, carriers are frozen out — only dopants contribute. At high T, thermal generation dominates and ni² = np. The dashed yellow line marks your current T.

Carrier Concentration n_i(T)

Intrinsic: n_i = √(N_c N_v) exp(-E_g/2k_BT).

Bandgap vs Temperature ⓘ
Varshni model
Lattice vibrations (phonons) reduce the effective periodic potential, shrinking the bandgap. Different materials have unique α and β coefficients.

Bandgap E_g(T)

Varshni fit: E_g(T) = E_g(0) - αT²/(T+β). Shrinks with phonons.

Conductivity vs Temperature ⓘ
Three conductivity regimes
Freeze-out: Low T, carriers bound to dopants → σ minimal.
Extrinsic: Mid T, dopants ionised, mobility still high → σ peaks.
Intrinsic: High T, ni dominates but μ falls via phonon scattering → σ plateaus or drops.

Intrinsic Conductivity σ(T)

σ = q(μ_e n + μ_h p). Thermally activated carriers dominate.

✨ Physics Insight: Temperature, Doping, and Fields

Temperature controls thermal carrier generation via the Varshni bandgap and the Boltzmann factor exp(−Eg/2kT).
Doping shifts the Fermi level: donors (ND) pull EF toward Ec; acceptors (NA) push it toward Ev.
Electric fields tilt the bands, causing electrons to drift in the conduction band and holes in the valence band.

$$E_g(T) = E_{g0} - \frac{\alpha T^2}{T + \beta} \quad \text{(Varshni)}$$ $$n_i = \sqrt{N_c N_v}\,e^{-E_g/2k_BT} \qquad E_F = E_i + k_BT\ln\frac{n}{n_i} \qquad \sigma = e(n\mu_e + p\mu_h)$$
