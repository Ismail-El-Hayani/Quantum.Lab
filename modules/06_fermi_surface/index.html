Derivative ∂f/∂E

Thermal broadening kernel. Width ≈ 3.5 k_B T around E_F.

−∂f/∂E is a thermal-broadened delta function centred on μ. Its width ~kBT. Only electrons within this window respond to external perturbations — the ``thermometer'' of the Fermi sea.

⚙ Applications & Extensions
Real consequences of Fermi-Dirac statistics in materials.

Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons share all four quantum numbers.

Pauli paramagnetic susceptibility χP(T) ~ μ(EF) in a Fermi liquid. Constant at low T; drops when kBT ~ EF.

Thermionic Emission J(T)

Richardson-Dushman: J = AT² e^(-φ/k_BT).

Thermionic emission current J = AT2exp(−φ/kBT). Only electrons in the high-energy tail escape the work-function barrier φ.

Work Function Distribution

Energy to lift an electron from E_F to vacuum.

White-dwarf degeneracy pressure P ∝ n5/3: the same Pauli pressure that keeps the star from collapsing is what prevents metals from collapsing under their own electronic charge.

💡 Physical Insights

The Fermi-Dirac distribution f(E) = [exp((E−μ)/kBT) + 1]−1 is the cornerstone of electron behaviour in solids. At absolute zero it is a perfect step function: all states below EF are filled, all above are empty. This Pauli blocking is why metals have Pauli paramagnetism, a specific heat proportional to T, and thermal conductivities carried almost entirely by electrons.

The finite-temperature correction to the chemical potential in 3D is μ(T) ≈ EF [1 − (π2/12)(kBT/EF)2], valid when T ≪ TF = EF/kB. Even at room temperature, typical metals have T/TF ~ 10−2, so only a tiny fraction of electrons within kBT of EF can participate in transport or thermal excitation. This explains why the electronic specific heat Cv = γT is so small compared with the classical value 3NkB.

The Wiedemann-Franz law κ/(σT) = L = (π2/3)(kB/e)2 = 2.44×10−8 WΩ/K2 holds because both charge and heat are carried by the same quasiparticles near EF. A Lorenz number close to this universal value is a fingerprint of Fermi-liquid behaviour. In semiconductors phonons carry heat while electrons carry charge, so L is violated.
