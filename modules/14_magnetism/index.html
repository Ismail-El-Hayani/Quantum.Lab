Magnetization vs Temperature

Spontaneous magnetization M_s(T) following mean-field theory: M ∝ √(T_c - T).

What it shows: Normalized magnetization M(T)/Ms versus temperature. Below Tc, exchange interactions win over thermal disorder and spontaneous order appears (Brillouin-like tanh law).
Why it matters: The M(T) knee is the smoking gun for a ferromagnetic transition and lets you read Tc off a magnetization experiment directly.

What it shows: The classic M–H hysteresis loop: sweep external field H up (green) and down (magenta). Captures remanence Mr, coercivity Hc, and saturation Ms. At T → Tc the loop collapses because thermal energy destroys domains.
Why it matters: The loop area and coercivity decide whether a material is a soft transformer core (low Hc) or a hard permanent magnet / storage medium (high Hc, high Mr).

✨ Why does this matter?

Magnetism arises from how atomic moments respond to external fields and to each other. In paramagnets, moments align against thermal disorder (χ ∝ 1/T). In ferromagnets, exchange interactions produce spontaneous order below Tc. Domains are the microscopic consequence: locally aligned regions that can cancel globally until a field aligns them. The hysteresis loop encodes memory: coercivity, remanence, and saturation determine whether a material is a soft or hard magnet.

$$\chi = \frac{C}{T - \theta}, \qquad M \propto \tanh\left(\frac{\mu H}{k_B T}\right), \qquad B = \mu_0(H + M)$$
