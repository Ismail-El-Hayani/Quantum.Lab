Thin Film Interference & Airy Formulas

A plane wave hits a thin film n2 of thickness d on substrate n3. Internal reflections interfere, giving oscillating reflectance and transmittance.

\(\displaystyle \beta = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\,n_2 d \cos\theta_2 \quad ; \quad \mathcal{R} = \frac{|r_{12} + r_{23} e^{2i\beta}|^2}{|1 + r_{12} r_{23} e^{2i\beta}|^2} \quad ; \quad \mathcal{T} = \frac{n_3\cos\theta_3}{n_1\cos\theta_1}\,|t|^2\)

📜 Fresnel Coefficients & Airy Summary

Interface 1→2:   \(r_{12} = \dfrac{n_1\cos\theta_1 - n_2\cos\theta_2}{n_1\cos\theta_1 + n_2\cos\theta_2}\)    \(t_{12} = \dfrac{2n_1\cos\theta_1}{n_1\cos\theta_1 + n_2\cos\theta_2}\)
Interface 2→3:   \(r_{23} = \dfrac{n_2\cos\theta_2 - n_3\cos\theta_3}{n_2\cos\theta_2 + n_3\cos\theta_3}\)    \(t_{23} = \dfrac{2n_2\cos\theta_2}{n_2\cos\theta_2 + n_3\cos\theta_3}\)
Total:   \(r = \dfrac{r_{12} + r_{23}e^{2i\beta}}{1 + r_{12}r_{23}e^{2i\beta}}\)    \(t = \dfrac{t_{12}t_{23}e^{i\beta}}{1 + r_{12}r_{23}e^{2i\beta}}\)

📖 Physical Motivation — Why does R oscillate?

Each time the wave reflects inside the film it picks up a round-trip phase \(2\beta = (4\pi/\lambda)\,n_2 d \cos\theta_2\). When \(2\beta = 2m\pi\) the internal reflections add constructively and R is maximum. When \(2\beta = (2m+1)\pi\) they cancel and R drops.

A quarter-wave anti-reflection coating satisfies \(n_2 = \sqrt{n_1 n_3}\) and \(d = \lambda/(4n_2)\), giving \(r_{12} = -r_{23}\) and \(\beta = \pi/2\) so the numerator of \(r\) vanishes.

🔬 Fabry–Pérot Resonance

When \(r_{23}\) is large (e.g. SiO₂ on Si) and \(2\beta = 2m\pi\), the denominator \(1 + r_{12}r_{23}e^{2i\beta}\) approaches its minimum, giving sharp transmission peaks. This is the principle of optical filters, VCSEL cavities, and spectroscopic etalons.
