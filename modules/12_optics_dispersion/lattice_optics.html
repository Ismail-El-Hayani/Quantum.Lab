🔍 Snell’s Law & Fresnel

When a photon crosses from air n₁=1 into a material n₂=n(E)+iκ(E):

n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂

The reflected intensity follows Fresnel. For complex n₂ the wave is inhomogeneous; we show the real-angle approximation for clarity, while the absorption coefficient κ controls the glow strength.

⚛️ Why does the outcome change?

Semiconductors / insulators: transparent below Eg (no states to absorb to). Above Eg, electrons jump across the gap — absorption dominates and no coherent refracted beam survives.

Metals: free electrons screen the field. Below the plasma edge the refractive index is imaginary → total reflection. Above the edge the metal becomes partially transparent.

🔊 Phonon Excitation & Lattice Heating

Every absorbed photon dumps a fraction of its energy into phonons (quantised lattice vibrations). The phonon bath temperature rises following the Einstein model: T_lat = 300 K + c·E_phonon.

As T_lat grows, atoms oscillate with larger amplitude (Debye–Waller factor), and free electrons in metals scatter more strongly off the distorted lattice → resistivity rises. Phonons anharmonically decay over ~1 s in this toy model.
