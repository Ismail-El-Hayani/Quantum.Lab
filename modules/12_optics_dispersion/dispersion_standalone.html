Dispersion

How the refractive index n and extinction coefficient κ depend on photon energy E (and thus on wavelength λ = hc/E).

5a. The Big Picture — Photon Interacts with a Material Slab

What the canvas shows

A photon packet (coloured by energy) hits a material slab. Depending on E and the material type:

  • Incident — packet travels from source toward the slab.
  • Reflected — electrons screen the E-field; packet bounces back.
  • Transmitted — below bandgap / above plasma edge, packet passes through.
  • Absorption glow — above-gap photons excite electron-hole pairs.
  • Phonon ring — energy deposited into lattice vibrations (NaCl).

The colour of the photon tracks its energy: red (IR, ~1 eV) → yellow-green (visible, ~2.5 eV) → violet (UV, >3 eV).

5b. Microscopic View — What Happens at the Atomic Scale?

Metal lattice (Cu, Ag, Al)

Ion cores sit in a free-electron sea. Below the plasma edge ωₚ, surface electrons screen the incoming E-field almost perfectly → total reflection. Above ωₚ the electrons cannot follow the field → partial transmission. This is the Drude model (Hummel Ch 11.1).

Semiconductor lattice (Si, GaAs)

Covalent bonds hold shared valence electrons. Below the bandgap E < E_g there are no empty states → the lattice is transparent. Above E_g a photon breaks a bond: a valence electron jumps to the conduction band, leaving a hole behind → strong absorption.

Insulator lattice (NaCl)

Ionic crystal with alternating Na⁺ and Cl⁻ sites. The bandgap is enormous (>8 eV) so visible photons cannot excite electrons across it. At far-IR energies phonon modes resonate → the lattice absorbs photons as quantized vibrations (Lorentz oscillator, Hummel Ch 11.5).

5c. Normal vs Anomalous Dispersion — n(E)

Why does n look like this?

The refractive index n(ω) is the real part of the complex dielectric function:

n(ω) = Re √ε(ω)      where ε(ω) = ε₁(ω) + i ε₂(ω)

Normal dispersion:    dn/dE < 0  (n decreases as E rises — most glasses)
Anomalous dispersion: dn/dE > 0  (near a resonance or absorption edge)

Near a strong resonance (e.g. NaCl phonon at ~0.02 eV) n diverges. In metals, ε₁ becomes negative below the plasma edge → n is imaginary → total reflection.

5d.1 Dielectric Function ε(E) = ε₁(E) + i ε₂(E)

Real part ε₁ = n² − κ² (cyan). Imaginary part ε₂ = 2nκ (pink). For a Drude metal: ε₁ → −∞ as E→0 and crosses zero at the plasma edge.

5d.2 Reflectivity, Transmittance, Absorption — R(E), T(E), A(E)

Below the bandgap / above the plasma edge: material is transparent (T ≈ 1). Near strong transitions: absorption dominates. Metals: R ≈ 1 below ωₚ.

5d.3 Reflected Colour — What Do You See?

How the colour is computed

The apparent colour comes from which wavelengths are reflected, not absorbed. Computed via CIE 1931 XYZ under D65 daylight:

X = Σ_λ R(λ)·I_D65(λ)·x̄(λ)
Y = Σ_λ R(λ)·I_D65(λ)·ȳ(λ)    -- luminance
Z = Σ_λ R(λ)·I_D65(λ)·z̄(λ)

R' = 3.2406·X − 1.5372·Y − 0.4986·Z   [IEC 61966-2-1 sRGB matrix]

Gold appears yellow-green in transmission → those colours are missing in reflection → appears reddish-yellow (Goethe / Hummel Ch 10.1).

5f. Physics Insights

Drude Model — Free-Envelope Electrons

For a metal with plasma frequency ωₚ and damping γ = 1/τ:

ε₁(ω) = 1 − ωₚ² / (ω² + γ²)
ε₂(ω) = ωₚ² γ / [ω(ω² + γ²)]

When ε₁ < 0 → n becomes imaginary → total reflection. The plasma edge marks the boundary between mirror and transparent behaviour.

Lorentz Oscillator — Bound Electrons

Classical harmonic oscillator with natural frequency ω₀:

ε(ω) = 1 + (Ne²/ε₀m)·(ω₀² − ω² − iγω)⁻¹

Near resonance n → ∞ (anomalous dispersion). This explains the sharp phonon absorption in ionic crystals like NaCl.

Kramers–Kronig Causality

Because the response must be causal, ε₁ and ε₂ are Hilbert-transform pairs. A peak in ε₂ at a given ω forces a rapid change in ε₁ nearby — this is why absorption and dispersion always go together.

Why Does Dispersion Matter?

• Chromatic aberration — lenses focus different colours to different points.
• Pulse broadening — in fibre optics, different wavelengths travel at different speeds.
• AR coatings — designed for a specific λ; performance degrades off-centre.
