✨ Why does this matter?

The QHO is the training gym of quantum mechanics. Its energy is quantized because confinement forces standing waves. Every n adds a node. The zero-point energy E₀ = ½ℏω means even at absolute zero, the particle still vibrates. This same math describes molecule vibrations (CO₂ at 2349 cm⁻¹), phonons in solids, and even the quantum uncertainty in microwave resonators.

$$H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2, \quad E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)$$

From the Schrödinger equation: For a particle in a potential V(x) = ½mω²x², we solve: $$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 \psi = E\psi$$ The solutions are Hermite polynomials H_n(y) × Gaussian e^{-y²/2}, where y = x√(mω/ℏ).

Parity: Even n → symmetric ψ(x). Odd n → antisymmetric. This is why selection rules in spectroscopy (Δn = ±1) exist.

Uncertainty: Δx·Δp = (n + ½)ℏ ≥ ℏ/2. At n = 0, Δx·Δp = ℏ/2 — the minimum uncertainty state. This is why the QHO ground state is also a coherent state in quantum optics.
