x = position in units of √(ℏ/mω) — the natural width of the ground-state Gaussian. The green dashed lines mark the classical turning points ±√(2E): the farthest a classical oscillator with the same energy would swing. Eigen mode: The wave is frozen in shape (|ψ|² gold does not move). Only Re and Im swap places as the global phase e⁻ⁱᴱᵗ/ℏ rotates. Super mode: Two eigenstates interfere. Coherent mode: The whole Gaussian packet slides back and forth like a classical ball on a spring, hitting the green turning points and rebounding.

✨ Why does this matter?

The QHO is the training gym of quantum mechanics. Its energy is quantized because confinement forces standing waves. Every n adds a node. The zero-point energy E₀ = ½ℏω means even at absolute zero, the particle still vibrates. The coherent state |α⟩ is a displaced Gaussian that oscillates back and forth like a classical particle — the closest quantum analog to a macroscopic oscillator.

$$H = \\frac{p^2}{2m} + \\frac{1}{2}m\\omega^2 x^2, \\quad E_n = \\hbar\\omega\\left(n + \\frac{1}{2}\\right), \\quad |\\alpha(t)\\rangle = |\\alpha(0)e^{-i\\omega t}\\rangle$$
Eigenstate |n⟩
Stationary state: ψ(x,t) = ψₙ(x)e⁻ⁱᴱⁿᵗ/ℏ. Re and Im oscillate in place. |ψ|² is time-independent.
Coherent state |α⟩
Displaced Gaussian: ⟨x(t)⟩ = x₀ cos(ωt). The packet shape doesn't spread — minimum uncertainty at all times.
