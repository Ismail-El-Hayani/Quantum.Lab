Zeeman Splitting

Zeeman Energy Splitting in a Magnetic Field

In an external magnetic field B, each orbital level l splits into 2l + 1 sub-levels labelled by magnetic quantum number m. The spacing ΔE = μBB is the same for every adjacent pair — the normal Zeeman effect. This is the origin of the anomalous Zeeman effect when spin is included.

✨ Theory: From Bohr to Schrödinger

The transition observed by clicking "Orbit Cloud" represents the shift from the Bohr model (classical particles in fixed rings) to the Quantum Mechanical model. In the latter, we describe electrons via the Wavefunction \(\psi\).

🎯 Challenge 2: Fine Structure Splitting Medium
Spin-orbit coupling splits each level into j = l ± ½. Identify which j is lower in energy for the n=2, l=1 state.
🏆 Reward: 75 XP · "Fine Structure Analyst" badge
What it shows: Spin-orbit coupling splits the n=2, l=1 level into j = l ± ½. The j = 1/2 state (lower) and j = 3/2 state (upper) are separated by a fine-structure energy ΔE𝞫 ≈ (Zα)⁴ 13.6 eV / n³. This splitting is the origin of the sodium doublet and the tiny energy differences in precise atomic clocks.
🏎 Challenge 3: Bohr Radius Scaling Hard
The Bohr radius scales as ⟨r⟩ ∝ n². If the ground state (n=1) has radius ~1.5 a₀, how many times larger is the n=3 state?
🏆 Reward: 100 XP + "Bohr Master" badge
What it shows: The Bohr radius scales as ⟨r⟩ = a₀ n² [1 + ½(1 − l(l+1)/n²)], so for any fixed l the dominant dependence is n². The n=3 state is therefore 9× larger than n=1 in spatial extent — a direct consequence of the Coulomb potential's 1/r form and the virial theorem. This scaling controls how tightly bound an electron is and therefore how strongly it interacts with external fields.

🧩 Puzzle 3: Hund' la Rule Ordering Master
Ordering electron configurations by energy is NOT simply by n. Click configurations in the correct filling order (lowest energy first).
🏆 Reward: 120 XP + "Hund' la Wizard" badge
What it shows: Hund's rules maximise total spin S (parallel spins first), then maximise orbital angular momentum L, and finally obey spin-orbit coupling J = |L − S| for less-than-half-filled shells. This ordering minimises Coulomb repulsion by keeping electrons spatially separated and explains the magnetic moments of the first-transition-series atoms.
