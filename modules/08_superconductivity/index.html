✨ Why does this matter?

Superconductivity is a macroscopic quantum phenomenon. Below Tc, electrons form Cooper pairs bound by phonon exchange. The energy gap 2Δ prevents pair-breaking by thermal energy. The Meissner effect (χ = -1) is not just zero resistance — it is active field expulsion. BCS theory predicts Δ(0) ≈ 1.76 k_B T_c. London theory gives λ_L = √(m/μ₀ n_s e²) for the magnetic penetration depth.

$$H_c(T) = H_0\left[1 - \frac{T^2}{T_c^2}\right], \quad \lambda_L = \sqrt{\frac{m}{\mu_0 n_s e^2}}, \quad \Delta(0) \approx 1.76\,k_B T_c$$

BCS ground state: All Cooper pairs occupy the same quantum state — a macroscopic wavefunction ψ = |ψ|e^{iφ}. The phase φ is what enables the Josephson effect and flux quantization.

Type I vs Type II: Type I expels all field below H_c. Type II allows partial penetration via Abrikosov vortices between H_{c1} and H_{c2}. NbTi and Nb₃Sn are Type II — essential for MRI magnets (up to 15 T).
