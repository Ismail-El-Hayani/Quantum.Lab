⚙ Controls
Barrier height V₀ 5.0

The potential energy inside each barrier. Higher V₀ deepens the wells and increases the energy cost for an electron to tunnel between neighboring cells. This widens the bandgaps and narrows the allowed bands, pushing the system toward discrete atomic levels.

Electron Energy E 2.0

Energy of the electron probing the single barrier (top-right plot). When E < V₀, the wave function decays exponentially inside the barrier — the tunneling "leakage" amplitude. Higher E increases transmission.

Barrier width b/a 0.20

Fraction of the lattice period occupied by the barrier. Larger b/a means thicker barriers → stronger confinement, narrower bands, and larger bandgaps. At b/a → 0 the bands become nearly free-electron-like; at b/a → 1 the system approaches isolated atoms with discrete levels.

Lattice constant a 1.0

Period of the repeating potential. Larger a lowers the Brillouin-zone boundary (π/a) and squeezes the E(k) plot horizontally. The ratio b/a matters more than a alone for the band structure shape.

Reduced zone: All bands folded into the first Brillouin zone [−π/a, +π/a]. Easier to see gaps. Extended zone: Bands drawn in their "natural" k regions, showing the free-electron parabola interrupted by Bragg reflections at zone boundaries.

✨ Physics Insight: From Single Barrier to Bloch Bands

The Tunneling Simulation (top-right) shows a single electron encountering one barrier. When E < V₀, the wave function decays exponentially inside the barrier; if the barrier is thin (small b/a), a non-zero amplitude "leaks" through to the far side. This is quantum mechanical tunneling — classically impossible, yet measurable in every semiconductor device.

The Band Structure (bottom plot) is the global consequence of this leakage repeated across a periodic lattice. High tunneling (thin barriers, low V₀) makes the atomic orbitals overlap strongly → broad energy bands. Low tunneling (thick barriers, high V₀) isolates the atoms → narrow bands that behave like discrete atomic levels. The gaps between bands are forbidden energies where no traveling wave can exist in the crystal.

Key controls: Drag V₀ to open/close gaps. Drag b/a to change barrier thickness. Toggle Reduced vs Extended zone to see how the same bands fold/unfold across the Brillouin zone.

$$\text{Transmission } T \approx e^{-2\kappa w} \quad \text{with} \quad \kappa = \sqrt{\frac{2m(V_0-E)}{\hbar^2}} \qquad \text{(WKB approximation)}$$

✨ Key Observations

Reflection & Interference: When the packet reaches the barrier, a portion bounces back. This reflected wave superposes with the still-arriving incident wave, producing standing interference fringes on the left (blue-red oscillations). The fringe spacing is λ/2, directly revealing the packet's de Broglie wavelength. Notice how the fringes grow sharper as the packet width σ increases (narrower momentum spread).

Transmission: Part of the packet carries past the barrier. If E > V₀, transmission is classical — the wave simply climbs over. If E < V₀ but the barrier is thin, transmission is purely quantum tunneling. The transmitted packet appears delayed and broadened because high-momentum components tunnel more easily than low-momentum ones — this is filtering in k-space.

Forbidden Leakage: When E < V₀, the barrier region is shaded pale yellow. Classically, no particle can exist here. Yet |ψ|² > 0 inside — the tunneling amplitude. The probability readout P(barrier) quantifies this "forbidden" population. In a periodic lattice, this same leakage, repeated cell after cell, is what creates energy bands and bandgaps.

Experiment: Try the Semicircle barrier for smooth, physical scattering. Compare with Square — you'll see extra ripples from the discontinuity. Switch to Gaussian for the smoothest, most realistic barrier shape. Then vary k₀: low energy (E ≪ V₀) gives nearly total reflection with tiny tunneling; high energy (E ≫ V₀) gives almost free passage.

Multiple barriers: Increase Barrier count to 3+ and watch the packet rattle between barriers like a photon in a Fabry-Pérot cavity. At certain spacings (when cavity width ≈ integer × λ/2) you'll see dramatically enhanced transmission — a precursor to the formation of Bloch bands in a periodic lattice. Reduce Barrier spacing until barriers overlap — the system approaches the single-barrier Kronig-Penney limit.

$$ i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi = \Bigl[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + V(x)\Bigr]\psi \qquad\text{with}\qquad \psi(x,0)=\Bigl(\frac{1}{2\pi\sigma^2}\Bigr)^{\!1/4}\exp\!\Bigl[-\frac{(x-x_0)^2}{4\sigma^2}+ik_0x\Bigr] $$
